    Chapter 1

    08:14. An invoice has disappeared.

    A client calls. They say their invoice is missing. The consultant opens SAP Business One and stares at a screen with thirty fields, four tabs and no answer.

    The system isn’t broken. The question is wrong. “Where is the invoice?” is not answered by looking at a screen: it’s answered by following a document back to the one that created it, and back to the one that created that.

    Those who don’t know that open eleven windows. Those who do open one and press a button.

    Chapter 2

    What it costs to ask the wrong question.

    The invoice appears. It was cancelled and replaced by another, in another series. The cost isn't the search: it's that nobody knew where to look.

    • 0.00 is what the aged receivables show for a draft of 500.00 that's been sitting there for three weeks. A draft doesn't post to the ledger and doesn't move stock: it's a frozen promise.
    • 856.80 is what the customer owes per the general ledger, spread across three lines that were created automatically when the invoice was added. None were typed by hand.

    Meanwhile the customer waits, the payment doesn't come in and the same question returns on Thursday. With a different document.

    Figures from the lab itself: the draft from SYN-SK-L0-08 and invoice 1001-2026.

    Chapter 3

    No screen. A path.

    Every document is born from another and leaves three footprints: it moves stock, writes a ledger entry, shifts the partner balance.

    Whoever knows those three footprints never memorises the screen. They read it.

    Now look at a real document.
