    The system isn’t broken.The question is wrong.

    A client calls. They say their invoice is missing. The consultant opens SAP Business One and stares at a screen with thirty fields, four tabs and no answer.

    “Where is the invoice?” is not answered by looking at a screen: it’s answered by following a document back to the one that created it, and back to the one that created that.

    Those who don’t know that open eleven windows. Those who do open one and press a button.

    CONSULTANT’S RULE 01

    Never search for a document before asking what created it.

    Chapter 2

    What it costs to ask the wrong question.

    The invoice appears. It was cancelled and replaced by another, in another series. The cost isn't the search: it's that nobody knew where to look.

    • 0.00is what the aged receivables show for a draft of 500.00 that's been sitting there for three weeks. A draft doesn't post to the ledger and doesn't move stock: it's a frozen promise.Draft→ no posting
    • 856.80is what the customer owes per the general ledger, spread across three lines that were created automatically when the invoice was added. None were typed by hand.A/R Invoice→ ledger

    Meanwhile the customer waits, the payment doesn't come in and the same question returns on Thursday. With a different document.

    Figures from the lab itself: the draft from SYN-SK-L0-08 and invoice 1001-2026.

    CONSULTANT’S RULE 02

    A document is defined as much by what it changes as by what it contains.

    Chapter 3

    No screen. A path.

    Each document completes its job, then hands the process to the next one. Nothing is crossed out; the chain keeps its history.

    Follow the handoff and the footprints become predictable: stock, ledger, balance.

    CONSULTANT’S RULE 03

    When you know the handoff, the next system effect becomes predictable.

    Enough theory. Follow the transaction.

    Now follow one transaction.
