Chapter 1

08:14. An invoice has disappeared.

A client calls. They say their invoice is missing. The consultant opens SAP Business One and stares at a screen with thirty fields, four tabs and no answer.

Something’s broken? No. The question is wrong. “Where is the invoice?” isn’t answered by staring at the screen — it’s answered by tracing a document back to the one that created it, and that one back to the one that created it.

Those who don’t know that open eleven windows. Those who do open one and press a button.

Chapter 2

What it costs to ask the wrong question.

The invoice shows up — but it’s been cancelled and replaced with a new one in a different series. The real cost isn’t the time spent searching. It’s that nobody knew where to look in the first place.

  • 0.00 is what the aged receivables show for a draft worth 500.00 that’s been sitting there for three weeks. A draft doesn’t post to the ledger and doesn’t move stock: it’s a frozen promise.
  • 856.80 is what the customer owes per the general ledger, spread across three lines created automatically when the invoice was added. None were typed by hand.

Meanwhile the customer waits, the payment doesn’t come in and the same question returns on Thursday — with a different document.

Figures from the lab: the draft from SYN-SK-L0-08 and invoice 1001-2026.

Every document comes from somewhere else and leaves three footprints: it changes inventory, it creates a ledger entry, it shifts a partner’s balance.

Whoever knows those three tracks doesn’t need to memorise the screen. They read it.

Take a breath. Look at a real document.
