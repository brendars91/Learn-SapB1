    Chapter 1

    08:14. An invoice has disappeared.

    A client calls. They say their invoice is missing. The consultant opens SAP Business One and stares at a screen with thirty fields, four tabs and no answer.

    Nothing's broken with the system. You're just asking the wrong question. "Where is the invoice?" is not answered by looking at a screen: it's answered by following a document back to the one that created it, and back to the one that created that.

    If you don't know this, you'll end up with eleven windows open. If you do? Just one window and a button press.

    Chapter 2

    What it costs to ask the wrong question.

    The invoice shows up - but it's been cancelled and replaced with a new one in a different series. The real cost isn't the time spent searching. It's that nobody knew where to look in the first place.

    • 0.00 is what the aged receivables show for a draft of 500.00 that has been sitting there for three weeks. A draft doesn't post to the ledger and doesn't move stock: it's a frozen promise.
    • 856.80 is what the customer owes per the general ledger, spread across three lines that were created automatically when the invoice was added. None were typed by hand.

    Meanwhile the customer waits, the payment doesn't come in and the same question returns on Thursday. With a different document.

    Figures from the lab materials: the draft from SYN-SK-L0-08 and the invoice 1001-2026.

    Chapter 3

    No screen. A path.

    Every document comes from somewhere else and leaves three little footprints behind: it changes inventory, it creates a ledger entry, and it shifts someone's balance.

    Whoever knows those three tracks doesn't need to memorise the screen. They read it.

    Go on - take a look at an actual document.
