    Chapter 1

    08:14. An invoice has disappeared.

    A client calls. They say their invoice is missing. The consultant opens SAP Business One and stares at a screen with thirty fields, four tabs and no answer.

    The system isn't broken. The question is wrong. "Where is the invoice?" is not answered by looking at a screen: it's answered by following a document back to the one that created it, and back to the one that created that.

    Those who don't know that open eleven windows. Those who do open one and press a button.

    Chapter 2

    What it costs to ask the wrong question.

    The invoice shows up. It was cancelled and replaced by another, in another series. The cost isn't the search: it's that nobody knew where to look.

    • 0.00 is what the aged receivables show for a draft of 500.00 that's been sitting there for three weeks. A draft doesn't post to the ledger and doesn't move stock: it's a frozen promise.
    • 856.80 is what the customer owes per the general ledger, spread across three lines created automatically when the invoice was added.

    The customer waits while the payment doesn't come in and the same question returns on Thursday with a different document.

    Figures from the lab: the draft from SYN-SK-L0-08 and invoice 1001-2026.

    Chapter 3

    No screen. A path.

    Every document comes from somewhere else and leaves three footprints: it changes inventory, it creates a ledger entry, it shifts a partner's balance.

    Whoever knows those three tracks doesn't need to memorise the screen. They read it.

    Take a breath. Look at a real document.

    The chain builds itself as you read. Five documents, three effects, one commercial event.
